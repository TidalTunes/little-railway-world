# Performance

The continental preset contains 256 cities, 287 rail links, 32 regional circuits, and 2,048 active three-car trains. It starts in the flat map. The larger preset accepts `?layout=stress&cities=512&trains=4096`; city counts must be multiples of eight. Requested fleets are limited by safe track capacity, rather than spawning overlapping trains.

## Reproduce

```sh
npm ci
npm test
npm run build
npm run preview
node --expose-gc scripts/benchmark-generation.mjs 3
node --expose-gc scripts/benchmark-traffic.mjs --stress
node scripts/benchmark-picking.mjs
node scripts/benchmark-indexing.mjs
node scripts/benchmark-scenery.mjs 3
```

Open the production preview with `?layout=stress&cities=256&trains=2048`. The canvas exposes diagnostics through `data-*` attributes without visible text: `cities`, `trains`, `active-trains`, `cpu-p95`, `frame-p95`, `draw-calls`, `triangles`, `geometries`, and detail cache/loading statistics, including `detail-workers` and `detail-worker-ms`. Frame and CPU percentiles use a rolling 180-frame window, published once a second. CPU time measures JavaScript plus WebGL submission, not GPU completion. Frame intervals measure requestAnimationFrame delivery, which also depends on display refresh and browser scheduling. Check steady state separately from initial loading and transitions.

## Measurements

Measure with the browser visibly presenting the game. A hidden desktop preview can throttle JavaScript after roughly a minute even while delivering animation callbacks; it produced misleading 35–45ms samples that immediately returned to approximately 6ms when shown, without reloading or changing the cache.

Measured on an Apple M4 Pro, macOS arm64, Node 24.14.0. These are local measurements, not guarantees for every device. Traffic measurements advance all requested trains, with exact carriage poses, safe headway, and crossing reservations enabled.

| Update median, 300 frames after warmup | 0.080ms | 0.359ms | 1.976ms |

The previous controller from commit `5a9c266` was compared with the same 256-city circle fixture; only fleet allocation was adapted to request identical counts. A single cold 60Hz update took 12.25 / 190.73 / 3,124.44ms at 128 / 512 / 2,048 trains. The replacement took 0.55 / 1.09 / 3.02ms. This isolates the old quadratic traffic cost; it is not a whole-browser speedup claim.

Full showcase generation fell from a profiled 3,253ms to approximately 539ms. The larger network-only plan takes approximately 991ms, validation 18.5ms, and a local 192-unit scenery plan 50.6ms. Geometry construction and tunnel excavation are additional worker work.

For a dense detailed chunk with 75,567 triangles, 400 identical selection rays produced identical nearest hits. Native raycasting p95 was 2.33ms; the transferred BVH reduced it to 0.024ms. BVH hydration took 0.11ms; construction runs in the worker.

Exact vertex indexing reduces a representative 486-asset cell's GPU attribute/index buffers from 9.87MB to 5.53MB (44%), and total geometry/BVH transfer data by 40%. Expanded triangle attributes remain bit-for-bit identical. The numeric hash-table pass takes approximately 10–23ms in the worker and also shortens BVH construction. Packets keep the original representation if indexing would increase their byte size.

In the 1280×720 browser preview, the 256-city / 2,048-train map uses 38 draw calls and approximately 60,633 triangles, with no detailed scenery allocated. Steady-state CPU p95 measured approximately 3.7–4.4ms and frame interval p95 approximately 8.4–8.7ms on this machine.

After adding blended terrain, the 512-city / 4,096-train map uses 34 draw calls and approximately 182,639 triangles. The complete Earth preset adds 96 selected cities and 250 trains on ten independent circuits. Global coastlines are tiled into 128-unit chart cells before triangulation, preventing continent-sized skinny triangles from exploding during spherical subdivision. Coast boundaries use eight-unit segments, broad ground interiors use 32-unit segments, and overlapping water/shore/contour layers retain sixteen-unit interiors. Indexed buffers stay indexed when batches join. The ocean uses a closed sphere rather than triangulating a planet-sized water polygon. Terrain colors and noise wrap continuously at the date line. Reusing prepared dry land and cutting only nearby river valleys reduced measured global terrain preparation from 16.55 seconds to 0.30 seconds; Europe keeps its existing clipping path. Preparation plus geometry compilation took 3.53 seconds, producing 868,724 terrain triangles in 22.10MB of buffers. All 132 mountain contour layers retained the previous visible coverage within numerical precision. The visible 716×831 production preview loaded the complete network in 18.22 seconds, including 17.98 seconds of worker preparation, and drew the globe in 30 calls with 958,557 triangles. The close isometric follow sample measured 1.1ms CPU p95 and 8.4ms frame intervals, with 1.04 million triangles in 53 calls; its first regional transition peaked at 10.4ms CPU. The 512-city / 4,096-train preset also loaded and kept its full fleet active. These are local checks at the stated viewport, not cross-device guarantees.

Terrain uses vertex colors in both representations, sampled from a continuous world-space field. Detailed chunk edges share canonical two-unit samples, so independent shoreline triangulations cannot create color seams. A 192-unit cell adds only 256 perimeter triangles in the regression fixture. Mountain vegetation shares the regional cache; farm variation stays inside the existing strict reservations. The denser farm fixture covers 26% more land with 10,890 triangles, slightly fewer than its previous 11,132.

The richer complete-globe land field uses a 320 × 161 RGB lookup (603.75 KiB). Spherical distance blending and broad vegetation variation are computed once per source site array; map and detail then sample the same field without scanning all sites for every vertex. A local color-only benchmark measured approximately 47ms initialization and 39ms for one million reused-target samples. This is not a whole-world load or rendering measurement. It adds no land geometry. Low mountain contours use the surrounding field at their true boundary and grade into a subtle highland tint over 48 chart units; the existing 41,525 low-contour vertices gain about 487 KiB of color data without adding triangles. Ocean colors use a shared spatial index of spherical coast segments for the continental shelf; the date line and pole cuts are excluded from shore calculations.

In the continuous-streaming iteration, a cold Earth zoom in the visible 861×831 preview peaked at 17.3ms CPU and 16.7ms frame interval after early shader preparation, down from a measured 109.2ms first-transition hitch before that preparation. The camera never stopped for chunk readiness. The settled follow view measured 0.9ms CPU p95 and 8.4ms frame intervals. A 512-city / 4,096-train overview still measured 5.6–6.2ms CPU p95 and 8.4ms frame intervals in a 1280×720 preview. These samples are local observations, not hardware-independent performance guarantees.

Scenery preparation now rejects distant polygon components and holes before exact membership or clipping work, builds large distance indexes lazily, and bounds footprint queries by the requested clearance. A sequential before/after benchmark used the same denser cities and fixed 192-unit cells; asset, mesh, and triangle counts were identical. Warm region planning plus mesh compilation fell from 1,912 to 577ms in Paris, 1,485 to 498ms in the Alps, 1,900 to 324ms in Brittany, and 491 to 150ms in the Sahara (66–83% less CPU time). Cold Paris detail fell from 3.75 to 1.22 seconds. Network planning alone fell from 15.68 to 12.91 seconds; overview geometry and browser loading are additional work. Run `scripts/benchmark-scenery.mjs` to measure both phases and output counts. Exact polygon union and footprint clearance semantics remain unchanged.

The residential-density iteration removes the Earth preset’s fixed eight-unit city radius and uses level-based footprints. Across the 96 source cities, buildings increase from 77 to 1,199 and houses from 66 to 774; median houses per city rise from one to eight. Seventy-seven cities gain houses. Terrain-constrained neighborhoods still skip unsafe lots. With the new radii and budgets held constant, the previous placement search produced 929 buildings in 7.27 seconds across 75 city regions; reusable frontage and infill candidates produce 1,199 in 7.30 seconds. These timings measure regional planning, excluding geometry compilation. A combined-region regression validates building and street clearances across the entire Earth city set.

## Architecture and limits

- Biome queries use cached polygon and hole bounds, lazy ring/edge indexes, and bounded exact clearance searches. Regional clipping passes only intersecting polygons and holes to the Boolean sweep, then retains the exact local outline intersection.
- Route and river queries use exact spatial indexes. Junction discovery prunes distant segments before intersection tests. Deterministic region generation reuses the same immutable network indexes.
- Traffic keeps ordered same-route leaders, indexed conflict blocks, and a persistent spatial hash of carriage footprints. Sampling uses exact line/arc segments with indexed distance lookup. A shared typed pose buffer feeds both representations.
- Terrain clipping and triangulation for the far map run in the worker and transfer ready-made position buffers. The far map uses coarse curve tessellation, batched city glyphs, and a single readable marker per train. Hidden map marker updates are skipped; the coarse terrain can remain beneath streaming detail.
- Detailed carriages share owner/cab templates, with instancing only for visible trains. Static colors become vertex colors, allowing a few shading batches per region instead of separate draws for every material color. Exact indexed vertices reduce buffer size without altering the art.
- World planning, asset placement, mountain CSG, color batching, and selection BVHs run in module workers. Devices reporting at least four logical processors use two detail workers; others use one. The immutable network is sent directly between workers through a MessageChannel, excluding overview GPU buffers, so its extra clone does not run on the render thread. At most two cells execute concurrently, with three regional requests allowed across compilation and upload. Obsolete queued requests are cancelled immediately; already executing results are discarded when obsolete. A failed optional worker falls back to the primary. Transferable geometry buffers avoid copying results to the main thread. Hydration has a per-frame CPU budget.
- Detail coverage includes the local-detail footprint and the proposed next-frame camera. Logical navigation runs before coverage selection. Map/detail blend is a pure function of displayed normalized zoom (0.78–1.0), with prefetch starting at 0.35. Loading readiness never holds the camera or changes the blend threshold. A complete coarse terrain surface remains underneath local detail, and uploaded regions reveal progressively. Fast zooms or pans into uncached areas can therefore show coarse ground while the worker prepares their scenery.
- Hydration and uploads remain bounded per frame. Each CPU-hydrated mesh uploads through a hidden 1×1 render pass, at most one mesh per frame. The pass submits one triangle to initialize its attribute and index buffers, then restores the original draw range, render target, and shadow state. A region is available only after every upload is acknowledged. Shader preparation initializes the world-light shadow targets, offscreen, shadow-depth (ordinary and instanced), and direct-render variants. Preparation begins when the world loads, before a user reaches the transition. Tiny retained surface and depth proxies warm future chunk and train programs without drawing terrain. Preparation restores visibility and renderer state between asynchronous steps. This spreads buffer upload work across frames instead of submitting every newly visible region at once.
- Complete-globe navigation wraps longitude and clips regional requests to the two adjacent date-line intervals. Panning and holding WASD update only the camera; they do not rebuild the globe. Keyboard velocity uses exponential acceleration and braking with integrated travel, so equal elapsed input gives the same motion at different frame rates. Diagonal input is normalized. Detailed dragging rotates around a fixed ground pivot, including during train follow; WASD moves that pivot with the spherical longitude metric. Taking over from a drag clears remaining inertia without applying its tail. Blur and tab hiding stop keyboard motion immediately.
- Spherical worlds retain chart-space region keys and simulation data. Camera rays intersect radial elevation shells to find the required geographic regions; physical bounds control frustum culling. Static vertices bend before BVH construction. Dynamic trains use a cached inverse geographic transform and its exact local derivative, so the same chart poses align with curved rails without rebuilding train geometry.
- The detail cache targets 128MiB and 48 chunks. Required cells and visible regions take priority and can exceed those targets; `budgetOverflow` reports byte overflow. GPU attribute storage is additional. Prefetch stops before exhausting the budget. Required viewport coverage has no default cell-count cutoff: polar charts, portrait screens, and shallow orbits can exceed the LRU target. An arbitrary cutoff would leave some visible regions permanently without detail. Physical chord lengths determine curvature padding, curved bounds reject out-of-view cells, and wholly ocean cells use the existing ocean shell. This keeps polar detail finite instead of expanding coverage across the entire chart.
- Shadows cover the local camera region. Far maps render directly without shadow passes or drawing into transition framebuffers; those targets are allocated ahead of time for the first zoom. Animation suspends in hidden tabs.

The stress preset uses separated regional circuits to demonstrate a large moving fleet. Dense shared junctions intentionally reduce throughput through reservations; arbitrary authored networks still have finite train capacity. The prototype has no economy or pathfinding game loop. Mobile hardware, unusual display resolutions, and very dense imported city layouts require their own profiling.
