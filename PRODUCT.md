# Little Railway World

## Users and purpose
A browser-based low-poly world, now beginning with a playable settlement loop on a compact flat island. You spawn as a station-passenger-sized traveller near one teal house in a forest clearing. Trees can be chopped with the axe: each falls, disappears, and awards 2–4 logs. Ten logs builds another house. A transparent cube checks terrain, clearance and affordability. The square lot follows your facing direction; its door chooses one of four sides, with a seeded probability favoring the settlement's center of mass. Placement fixes that orientation. Larger numeric log and gold balances sit directly over the landscape, accompanied by small faceted timber and coin symbols. Rust identifies timber and chopping, gold identifies currency, and owner teal identifies city growth and building. The city level remains a numeral on a painted badge with progress toward the next level; colored action icons and keycaps keep controls compact, with names available on hover or keyboard focus. A small sun or moon accompanies the current phase. Gameplay state lasts for the current session.

New houses and civic buildings reveal upward from their foundations over two seconds, accompanied by a small burst of pale construction dust. Placement, paths and resources take effect immediately; the reveal is cosmetic and becomes instant with reduced motion.

Three nearby homes form a city and create their connecting footpaths; one or two homes remain an outpost without roads. City levels 1–5 begin at 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 homes, adding a well, market, water tower, fountain plaza and town hall respectively. A level numeral identifies each city from above, while its landmarks identify progress at ground level. Earned landmarks wait for a clear, connected site and do not count as homes. Ordinary player progression excludes train stations; neutral villages can use other civic assets from the same catalog. Each home in a city has one resident, including the first three when the city forms. Residents are cosmetic: they take short trips to civic landmarks and neighboring homes, pause together, and return home at night, all on their own city’s actual paths. They walk through their doorways and stay hidden indoors until staggered departures at dawn.

Eligible farmland regions contain small neutral villages, each with three gray homes, one civic landmark and a level-1 marker. Seeded lane arrangements, uneven house frontages and staggered fields vary each hamlet. Civic landmarks include wells, markets, chapels, clock towers and water towers. Their gray residents visit nearby farms during the day and return indoors at night. They remain separate from the player's settlement, even when neighboring houses are built.

Three rival settlers on the default island act as stand-ins for future human players. Coral, gold and blue travellers start beside their own homes, walk to real trees, swing their axes for the same 2–4 logs and pay ten logs per new home. Their cities gain the same paths, residents and five civic milestones. Each stops expanding at fifteen homes and level 5. They keep working offscreen without loading scenery meshes. Ownership, money and city membership stay separate; harvested trees and occupied ground are shared.

At sunset, every player-owned house in a formed city earns one gold. Neutral villages, outposts, civic buildings and resident counts do not earn or alter player income. Third person shows the total in a gold notification across the screen; overview shows a small coin rising above each earning house. The calendar records paid days, so revisiting a view or processing another frame cannot pay the same day twice.

The same world supports third-person walking, an isometric miniature and a simplified map. V switches between the traveller and overview. Clicking the third-person scene captures the mouse for continuous relative look without dragging or screen-edge limits; Tab releases it for the corner controls. Touch and pen retain drag look, with arrow keys as a keyboard alternative. WASD moves, Shift runs, Space jumps, and E or a click chops. Houses can only be built in third person: B selects a cube directly ahead, turning aims it, looking down brings it closer, and looking up moves it farther within building reach. The camera heading aligns the square lot; click or Enter builds and Escape cancels. Building and overview have distinct cursors, while walking hides the captured pointer. The complete Earth and earlier populated island remain explicit art presets.

## Direction from the brief
Low-poly trains, isometric trees, miniature cities connected by working railway routes, and small details that make the landscape feel alive. Publish iterations to the existing GitHub Pages site. Support authored city and route layouts subject to geometric feasibility, automatic river bridges and mountain tunnels, and strict asset clearance. Let users zoom out to understand the network and select a train to follow it closely.

The default settlement island starts with one player home and neutral farming villages, without stations, tracks or trains. Its mountain spine, river and inland lake, sheltered bays, cultivated valleys, woodland and dune coast reuse the authored island geography. It opens directly in third person beside the starting home. The earlier eight-town railway island remains at `?layout=island`. The geography stays fixed when scenery is regenerated, giving future game systems a repeatable test environment. The saved Earth map remains at `?layout=earth`, with its 96 cities, 250 trains, real coastlines and polar terrain. The `earth-art-v1` Git tag preserves the complete version before this switch. Europe, the sixteen-city showcase, and stress presets also remain available.

## Art decisions
A painted model railway with warm daylight and moonlit nights: colored meadows, a blue river, compact towns, mountain forest, farms, and simple stone bridges. Day and night automatically last five minutes each, with gradual dusk and dawn. Sunlight and moonlight travel across the local sky, changing the direction and length of shadows; direct light fades at the horizon while ambient light keeps the world legible. N or the day/night button advances to the next phase for testing; it moves the calendar forward and settles sunset income normally. Keep the scene legible when zoomed out to many cities and trains. The emotional goals are curiosity, calm, and delight. The trains are the focal moving objects.

At the farthest distance, the same geography becomes a sparse network map: broad terrain and water regions, restrained mountain contours, railways and live trains. Playable cities use owner-colored level numerals 1–5 in place of building logos; outposts have no city marker. Individual scenery is reserved for closer views. The close camera looks down on the miniature and follows a selected train without a separate simulation or visible control panel.

Cities develop from compact settlements along winding lanes into dense neighborhoods with apartments, civic buildings, plazas, and towers. Farmland forms coherent groups of neighboring cultivated plots. Player color families carry consistently through their architecture and trains. Ownership and level remain editable authoring data in the saved art presets. The default island starts smaller, with resource gathering and individual house construction.

## Design principles

- The whole viewport is the world.
- A believable miniature, with carefully made silhouettes and details.
- Motion reveals the geography and connects places.
- The far map prioritizes cities, ownership, and connections over decorative detail.
- Keep gameplay controls legible, transparent and contextual; the landscape remains the main surface.
- Let the user inspect the art with discreet camera gestures.

## Accessibility
Provide a canvas description, visible keyboard focus, and keyboard equivalents for camera and train selection. + / − zoom; WASD pans in four directions; arrow keys orbit the world or pan map and close views; F follows the visible train nearest the centre; [ / ] cycle active trains; Escape leaves follow; Home resets; Space pauses motion in strategy views and jumps in third person; V toggles walking; Shift runs; E or a click/tap swings the axe in third person. B selects house placement only in third person, Enter builds and Escape cancels. In overview, T selects rail planning and Enter confirms the selected route. R regenerates only in the saved art presets. N advances to the next day or night for testing. Tab releases a captured mouse and retains normal focus navigation. Escape first cancels the current tool or frees the captured mouse before leaving walking. Click and touch selection distinguish taps from drags and multi-touch gestures.

Respect reduced motion by starting motion paused and making camera transitions and construction reveals immediate. Suspend animation while the tab is hidden; the day/night calendar continues independently of motion pause and catches up on return. Placement validity and settlement membership use short status text as well as color. Controls pair colored action icons with visible keycaps, accessible names, focus states and announcements. Action names appear on hover or focus; short contextual guidance and resource costs appear only while placing a house or railway. Numeric resources, city level and growth progress lead the interface, with a few colored resource and time-of-day symbols supporting the labels. Context text and tooltips retain high-contrast neutral ink, with color on action icons and keycaps. There is no separate house-count icon; city progress communicates development.

## Player-built railways

Overview offers T Railway and V Walk. A formed player city can connect to another formed city, including a gray village or rival settlement, for five gold. Select your city, hover a destination to preview a direct feasible route, then click to keep that destination selected. Evenly spaced route dots can be dragged to reshape its curves; Enter confirms the current valid preview and Escape cancels. Invalid or unaffordable routes appear red and cannot be built. Planning runs in a worker while the camera remains responsive. Each endpoint receives a station in its city’s color with an access path. Later connections can use either end of a station and share established approaches through tangent-matched switches. Houses and bot construction respect committed infrastructure. Construction clears intersected trees, rocks, farm estates and other scenery without granting logs, while protecting buildings. Water crossings receive bridges; mountains receive tunnels where there is enough rock cover and open cuts elsewhere.
